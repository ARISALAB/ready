HACCP for restaurants in Greece (2026): your EFET category, the records you need and free forms

Which category your venue falls into after the Hellenic Food Authority's 2026 circular, which records inspectors ask to see, what a new restaurant must have on file from day one, and a free set of printable forms.

The short answerEvery food business in Greece must apply self-checks based on HACCP principles (EU Regulation 852/2004). After EFET's February 2026 circular, most cafés, bars, independent restaurants and tavernas do not need a full HACCP system, but they must keep basic hygiene records and their staff must complete EFET's training programmes. Large chains and venues serving over 250 covers a day need a fully developed HACCP system.

A health inspection in Greece is not judged only by how clean the kitchen looks. It is judged by whether you can prove that you do things right every day: that you check temperatures, inspect deliveries, clean on a schedule and know what to do when something goes wrong. Your records are that proof.

If you are a foreign owner or investor, this is one of the areas where local practice matters most, and where clear information in English is hardest to find. Below you will find which category you belong to, which records you need, and at the end 8 ready-made forms to get started.

Is HACCP mandatory in Greece?

Yes, with a distinction that confuses many people. Regulation (EC) 852/2004 requires every food business to apply self-check procedures based on HACCP principles. That applies to everyone.

Certification (for example ISO 22000) is not mandatory. And not every venue needs the same depth of system: a small café does not have the same obligations as a 300-cover restaurant. That is where the categories of EFET (ΕΦΕΤ, the Hellenic Food Authority) come in.

Which category is your venue?

EFET's clarifying circular (ref. 1497/11.02.2026) classifies food businesses by risk. Your category can move up because of size or the population you serve:

CategoryWhich venuesWhat it means
1 · lowCafés, traditional kafeneia, bars, snack bars, mobile canteens, even in a chainNo full HACCP. EFET training programmes are mandatory.
2a · mediumRestaurants, tavernas, pizzerias, fast food and grill houses that are not part of a large chainNo full HACCP. EFET training programmes are mandatory.
3 · highRestaurants of large chains, in campsites, hospitals or educational institutions, or serving a large population (e.g. more than 250 covers a day)Fully developed HACCP with documented in-house training.

Quick check

Which category are you?

1. What kind of venue do you run?

2. Is it part of a large chain, or does it operate inside a campsite, hospital or educational institution?

3. Do you serve more than 250 covers a day?

If you are not sure about your category, the food technologist who prepares your study or the competent authority can confirm it.

The records you must keep

Whatever your category, the inspector will ask to see a folder with the following. How detailed it is depends on your category.

  • Self-check procedures or a HACCP study, adapted to your own venue.
  • Fridge and freezer temperatures, every day. This is one of the first things checked.
  • Goods received: what arrived, from whom, in what condition.
  • Cleaning and disinfection schedule, together with the safety data sheets (SDS) of the products you use.
  • Pest control: contract with a licensed company, visit reports, bait map. Missing documentation counts as a violation even if no pest has ever been seen.
  • Water file: mandatory for every food business, with proof of water supply and, if you have a tank, filters, an ice machine or a borehole, the matching records.
  • Staff training, with certificates.
  • Non-conformities and corrective actions: what went out of limits and what you did about it.
  • Allergen information for your dishes, under Regulation (EU) 1169/2011.

Indicative temperature limitsChilled 1 to 5 °C, frozen −18 °C or colder, hot holding above 60 °C. The limits that apply to you are the ones set in your own self-check procedures.

Opening now? Documents for day one

A new venue can be inspected in its first weeks. Besides hygiene records, the shop folder must hold the operating and staff documents. The list is indicative: exactly what you need depends on the municipality, the size and the activity.

Operating the venue

  • Proof of submission of the business notification (γνωστοποίηση) on notifybusiness.gov.gr, with a copy of the notification itself.
  • Proof of payment of the municipal fee (παράβολο). Without it, operation does not start legally.
  • Scaled floor plan of the premises, with the square metres of each area.
  • Solemn declaration of the person responsible for hygiene, if this is not the person who filed the notification.
  • Fire safety: certificate or whatever applies to your size, maintenance log for fire safety equipment and extinguisher refills.
  • Notification of music use to the municipality, if you play music.
  • Permit for use of public space, if you have tables and chairs outside.

Staff

  • Health certificate (πιστοποιητικό υγείας) for every employee and for the employer. It is valid for five years and is issued after a medical examination and a chest X-ray. Food handlers also take stool culture and parasitology tests.
  • Food hygiene training certificates, according to your category.
  • Staff schedule posted in a visible place.
  • Safety technician and the related records.

Hygiene and customers

  • The records from the previous section, in use from day one, not from the first inspection.
  • Supplier list, with invoices and delivery notes, for traceability of raw materials.
  • Price list available to customers before they order, and allergen information.

All of this is collected in a printable checklist, Form 00, which is also in the folder below. For every step of opening, from finding premises to the trial run, see our guide on how to open a restaurant in Greece.

What inspectors actually check

Hygiene is only one of the checks a Greek venue faces. Our free 2-minute health inspection risk check shows how exposed your venue is overall.

  • Whether records are filled in when the checks happen. Sheets in the same pen, the same handwriting and perfect numbers for the whole month show they were written in one go.
  • Whether the numbers match reality. If the sheet always says 3 °C and the thermometer shows 9 °C on inspection day, the problem is double.
  • What you did when something went out of limits. A deviation with a written corrective action shows the system works. A year of records with no deviation at all convinces nobody.
  • Whether staff know what they are doing. The inspector may ask the cook where temperatures are recorded, or what happens with a delivery that arrives too warm.
  • Whether everything is in one place. One folder in the venue, not papers spread across drawers and the accountant's inbox.

6 common mistakes

1. A study on the shelf, nothing in the kitchen

The study was done years ago, the folder looks perfect, but nobody fills in anything. A study without records proves nothing.

2. Filling everything in at the end of the month

The quickest way to lose the credibility of the whole folder. Hang the temperature sheet next to the fridges and decide who fills it in on each shift.

3. No corrective actions

When a fridge rises to 8 °C, writing down 8 is not enough. Write down what happened too: adjustment, products moved, technician called, products discarded.

4. Forgetting the water file

It is mandatory for everyone and usually missing. A water bill in the folder is the start. If you have a tank or an ice machine, you also need their cleaning records.

5. Training without paperwork

Your cook may know the job better than anyone, but without a training certificate in the folder, for the inspection they have not been trained.

6. New seasonal staff with no briefing

In high season, people join who do not know where the forms are. Five minutes of briefing on the first day, and a signature in the training record, solve the problem.

Frequently asked questions

Does my restaurant in Greece need HACCP or ISO 22000 certification?

No. Certification is optional. What is mandatory is applying self-check procedures based on HACCP principles, under Article 5 of Regulation (EC) 852/2004, and keeping the records that prove it.

Is a full HACCP system required for a small restaurant or café?

Usually not. Under EFET's 2026 circular, cafés and bars (category 1) and independent restaurants, tavernas and pizzerias (category 2a) do not need a full HACCP system, but must keep basic hygiene records and complete EFET's training programmes. Large chains and venues serving over 250 covers a day (category 3) need a fully developed system.

Are the forms enough to pass an inspection?

No. The forms are record-keeping templates. You also need your own venue's self-check procedures, adapted to your menu, equipment and premises, and the limits on the forms must match them.

Which staff need training?

Everyone who handles food. For cafés and bars (category 1) and for restaurants, tavernas and pizzerias outside large chains (category 2a), EFET's 2026 circular states that EFET's training programmes are mandatory.

Can I keep my hygiene records in English?

The records are there for your team and the Greek inspector, so most venues keep them in Greek. If your staff work in another language, ask your food technologist how to set up records the inspector will accept.

How long do I keep completed forms?

The period is set by your self-check procedures. In practice, keep at least the last year of completed forms in one folder at the venue, ready for the inspector.
