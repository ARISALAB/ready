    How the score works

    Every question has a weight: 3 for findings that bring the largest fines or can close the venue (staff declarations, the work card, receipts, traceability, the self-check file, fire safety and the notification itself), 2 for the hygiene points an inspector checks on most visits, and 1 for the rest. Your score is the share of points you have, out of the questions that apply to you. “Doesn’t apply” takes a question out of the count, “Not sure” keeps it in as a gap.

    The result is “Close to ready” only with 85 or more and no high-risk gap. One high-risk gap is enough to keep you out of that band, however good the rest is, because that single finding can cost more than everything else together.

    Where the fine amounts come from

    Each gap shows the fine set by law and a link to the source, as of November 2026:

    • Food hygiene: Law 5243/2025, article 104. Self-check procedures carry €500 to €5,000, traceability €500 to €20,000, and other breaches €200 to €5,000.
    • Operating notification: Law 4442/2016, article 15, from €100 to €20,000 for operating without notification or with changes that were not notified.
    • Staff: €10,500 for each undeclared worker, up to €31,500 on a repeat, and €10,500 for each employee without an active digital work card (Law 4808/2021).
    • Receipts: under AADE decision A.1115/2026, the venue closes at once for 48 hours if more than 10 receipts were not issued, or if the value not declared is over €500, even for a single receipt. A repeat means 96 hours.

    Two points are our own reading, and we say so: health certificates, staff training, temperature logs, labels, pest control, cleaning and hand basins are hygiene requirements under EU Regulation 852/2004, so we show them under the €500 to €5,000 range. Allergen information falls under the general €200 to €5,000 range. For fire safety, outdoor tables, music and the price list we found no fixed published range, so we show who sets the fine instead of guessing a number.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who inspects restaurants in Greece?
    Food hygiene is checked by EFET, the Hellenic Food Authority, and by the public health services of the regions. Staff are checked by the Labour Inspectorate, receipts by AADE, the tax authority, and the municipality checks the operating notification, outdoor tables and music. They work separately, so one visit can be followed by another.

    Is anything I enter saved or sent to you?
    Your answers stay in your browser while you use the check. Only if you ask for the full report by email do we receive your email address, the venue name if you give one, and your answers, so we can send it. You can ask us to delete them at any time.

    Are the fines in the results exact?
    No. They are the ranges set by law, as of November 2026. The inspecting authority sets the exact amount, depending on how serious the breach is and whether it is repeated.

    Does a high score mean I will pass an inspection?
    Not on its own. The check covers the points that most often lead to fines, not every line of the official inspection forms. It shows where you stand, it does not replace an inspection.
